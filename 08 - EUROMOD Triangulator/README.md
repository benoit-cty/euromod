
## Can we use it for Nòmos ?

Yes — quite a few. I went through the whole prototype (its PLAN.md, CLAUDE.md, the two skill ecosystems, scripts, and output data), and beyond the law-reference extraction there are at least seven things worth harvesting. Ranked by how much work they'd save us:

1. The per-country scrapers in the law-scraper skill — probably the biggest win. Under [law-scraper/scripts/](08 - EUROMOD Triangulator/.claude/skills/law-scraper/scripts/) there are ~20 production-tested scrape_CC.py scripts, one per member state, plus utilities encoding an anti-bot escalation ladder (httpx → curl_cffi impersonating Chrome → Playwright → Wayback Machine fallback). Our Nomotheca design says "adding a country is an adapter, not a rewrite" — these scripts are half-written fetcher adapters for all 27 countries, with the portal-specific knowledge (which sites need JS, which return 403, which have usable APIs) already paid for. Even the failures are valuable: their PENDING.md files document exactly which portals (legifrance, e-tar.lt, *.public.lu) resist automation, which tells us where our FR/LT/LU adapters need special handling.

2. All 27 Country Reports already converted to Markdown in [country-reports/Y16/](08 - EUROMOD Triangulator/country-reports/Y16/). Their CLAUDE.md makes an insight explicit that matters for us: the Country Reports are the natural translation layer between cryptic parameter acronyms ($tscse_minThres2, b_ch_amt) and semantic, searchable descriptions. Our Nomoscope frame step could look up the parameter in the CR to build much better retrieval queries — especially in the native language. That directly attacks our main failure mode (retrieval misses).

3. Discovery loops for corpus gaps — [search_augmentation.py](08 - EUROMOD Triangulator/.claude/skills/law-scraper/scripts/search_augmentation.py) (bottom-up: missing parameter → LLM formulates native-language queries → Exa/Brave search restricted to site:gov.xx) and [ghost_law_discovery.py](08 - EUROMOD Triangulator/.claude/skills/law-scraper/scripts/ghost_law_discovery.py) (top-down: CR section headers → find the foundational consolidated laws). This is essentially a prototype of the "cache-miss ingestion workflow" our architecture already anticipates: when retrieve finds nothing, formulate a search and feed ingest_citation. Their search-engine selection notes (Exa for finding official PDFs, Brave for strict site: queries, Tavily only for sanity-checks) are directly reusable.

4. Triangulation results as golden-set seed material. The [triangulations/J2.0+/](08 - EUROMOD Triangulator/triangulations/J2.0+/) inference JSONs contain per-parameter cross-checks (XML value vs CR value vs legislation value) for 7 countries. Parameters where all three sources agree are high-confidence golden cases for Nomokrisis; the flagged discrepancies are exactly the "hard cases" an eval set needs. Their "Not Found in legislation" lists are also a ready-made corpus-gap audit.

5. EUROMOD XML parsing know-how. The euromod-xml skill references and [extract_rules.py](08 - EUROMOD Triangulator/.claude/skills/euromod-policy-verification/scripts/extract_rules.py) encode hard-won gotchas: use lxml (files up to 116MB), properties are child elements not attributes, the namespace, extracting switch status. And two rules that belong in our Nomokrisis value scoring if they're not there yet: 99999999999 is an instrumental value meaning infinity (must be excluded from comparisons), and the June 30th rule — a policy year reflects the law as of June 30th, not January 1st. That second one should inform how we set/interpret as_of throughout the pipeline.

6. Ingestion quality-control heuristics. Their task2_verify.py flags scraped files that are too short, contain 404/403 text, are unrendered JS boilerplate, or are only a table of contents. That's a cheap QA gate we could add after Nomotheca's parse step, before chunks reach the DB — their experience shows roughly 25% of files (293/1034) had such problems.

7. Longer-term: the euromod-policy-implementation skill (system cloning, applying DefConst changes to the XML). We must stay export-first and human-gated, but after a human Accept in the UI, this defines what a machine-applicable export format looks like — worth aligning our export schema with it so JRC can apply accepted values downstream.

One caveat on your original idea: the [legislation-sources/](08 - EUROMOD Triangulator/legislation-sources/) URL lists are a good DB seed but are noisy — they mix actual legislation with DREES statistics pages, Eurostat tables, and press releases (visible in FR.md), and their own quality check flagged many scraped files as junk. Their corpus is also a current snapshot, not temporally versioned like ours. So I'd treat their URLs as ingestion candidates routed through our archive-first pipeline with a primary-vs-secondary source classification (their "Legislation is King" hierarchy maps nicely to a source-trust field in our schema), rather than bulk-importing their scraped Markdown.
